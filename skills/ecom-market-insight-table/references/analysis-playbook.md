# Analysis Playbook

## Table Insight Loop

Use this loop for reports and longer chat answers.

### 1. Evidence Gate

Start with what is visible:

- sample count
- source/platform
- provided fields
- missing fields
- sample-size grade
- strongest limitation

Never bury the limitation at the end if it changes the conclusion.

### 2. Segment Map

Build segments from category fields first. If category fields are missing, use recurring title terms and mark them as title-inferred.

Look for:

- product form: set, refill, portable, household, professional
- use scene: home, office, travel, children, pet, outdoor
- buyer problem: save time, storage, safety, comfort, taste, appearance
- specification: size, count, capacity, material, function

### 3. Demand Language

High-frequency title terms are not automatically opportunities. Classify them:

- **Access words**: nearly everyone uses them; buyers expect them.
- **Proof words**: reduce risk, such as official, certified, tested, ingredient, material.
- **Scene words**: indicate who or where, such as dormitory, baby, office, camping.
- **Outcome words**: buyer result, such as save space, no odor, fast, quiet.
- **Noise words**: promotional filler that does not sharpen positioning.

Good opportunity language is not just frequent. It is frequent enough to be recognized and specific enough to separate one offer from another.

### 4. Price Ladder

Use price bands as market behavior clues, not automatic strategy.

Read each band by:

- row count share
- average sales signal when available
- dominant title words
- shop/brand mix
- obvious offer difference

Recommended labels:

- **Mainstream band**: many rows and visible demand.
- **Crowded low band**: many rows, weak difference, price pressure.
- **Premium proof band**: higher price plus stronger brand/spec/proof.
- **Thin test band**: few rows but repeated demand signal; requires manual validation.
- **Unproven gap**: few rows and no sales signal; do not call it an opportunity yet.

### 5. Seller Map

Use brand/shop concentration to understand entry pressure.

- If one shop/brand dominates, treat it as a trust or supply barrier.
- If top sellers are fragmented, differentiation may matter more than brand defense.
- If many shops repeat similar titles, the category may be copy-heavy.
- If the table includes Tmall/JD flagship or authorized shops, separate them from marketplace sellers.

Use HHI or top-share as a guide, but explain it plainly.

### 6. Claim Saturation

Repeated selling points can mean two opposite things:

- Buyers expect the claim, so omitting it hurts conversion.
- The claim is crowded, so leading with it may not create a click reason.

Classify claims:

- **Must-have baseline**: common and buyer-relevant.
- **Crowded claim**: common but weakly differentiating.
- **Proof-needed claim**: useful but risky without evidence.
- **Underused angle**: appears less often but connects to a real scene or pain.

Do not recommend copying a claim if the product cannot prove it.

### 7. First Test Decision

End with one of five decisions:

| Decision | Use When | Next Action |
|---|---|---|
| enter narrowly | sample shows demand and a clear segment gap | test one segment, price, and proof point |
| wait and collect | signal exists but fields are missing | collect reviews, images, and more rows |
| avoid generic entry | low-price/crowded claims dominate | do not launch a me-too offer |
| reposition current product | user's product exists but title/price/proof mismatch | rewrite title, main image, or page promise |
| split the market | broad category hides different buyer scenes | create 2-3 subcategory briefs |

## Scoring

Use scoring only as a conversation aid, not a false precision model.

| Dimension | 0-5 Question |
|---|---|
| evidence quality | Are title, price, seller, and demand signals present? |
| demand visibility | Is there repeated buyer language or sales signal? |
| crowding pressure | Are price and claims highly repetitive? Lower score means more crowded. |
| differentiation room | Is there a segment or proof angle not fully saturated? |
| execution clarity | Can the next test be stated in one concrete action? |

Report the score as a directional read, for example `18/25, usable but needs review data before launch`.

## Forbidden Moves

- Do not say "必做", "稳赢", "蓝海金矿", or "保证转化".
- Do not treat sales rank, monthly sales, or review count as exact truth unless the source is known.
- Do not infer buyer demographics from titles alone without marking it title-inferred.
- Do not recommend claims involving medical, safety, efficacy, certification, or regulated categories without proof review.
